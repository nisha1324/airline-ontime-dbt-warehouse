## 2. Why the grain matters: the double-counting trap

Loaded as-is into a BI tool, a `SUM(Sectors_Scheduled)` card adds all four grains together. Over the full period:

| Measure | Flights scheduled | vs national total |
|---|---|---|
| Sum over every row of the raw file | 40,359,003 | 3.51× |
| National total (`network_all_airlines` rows) | 11,495,634.50 | 1.00× |
| Sum of route × airline rows only | 8,681,901 | 0.76× |

- The naive sum overstates the national schedule **3.51×**.
- Summing only the detailed route rows **understates** it: the published routes carry 76% of national flights. BITRE lists only competitive routes with 8,000+ passengers a month, so network totals have to come from the network rows, not from adding up routes.
- Ratios move less but still move: on-time arrivals over every row come to 80.24%, against 80.47% from the national rows.

## 4. Source defects

Flagged in staging, never silently fixed. These are the dbt tests that warn instead of failing; the structural tests (grain labels, keys, month continuity, routes within networks) all pass.

| Check | Rows | Years | Airlines |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cancellations missing | 316 | 2006, 2007 | Jetstar, Qantas, QantasLink |
| Departures delayed missing | 5 | 2005, 2007 | Jetstar, Qantas |
| Scheduled ≠ flown + cancelled | 35 | 2004, 2005 | All Airlines, Jetstar, QantasLink, Regional Express, Skywest |
| Flown ≠ departures on time + delayed | 46 | 2004, 2005, 2011, 2025 | All Airlines, Jetstar, Qantas, QantasLink, Regional Express, Skywest, SmartLynx Australia, Tigerair Australia |
| Flown ≠ arrivals on time + delayed | 51 | 2004, 2011, 2025 | All Airlines, Jetstar, Qantas, QantasLink, Regional Express, Skywest, SmartLynx Australia, Virgin Australia |
| Fractional counts | 56 | 2009, 2011 | All Airlines, Skywest, Tigerair Australia |

- Arrivals can legitimately fall short of flights flown: diverted flights count as neither on time nor delayed.
- Fractional counts (e.g. 33,233.46 departures on time) aren't explained in BITRE's notes; they are kept as published, so totals for 2009 and 2011 can end in a fraction of a flight.

### National totals that leave out an airline

The national row should equal the sum of the airline network rows. It does in 268 of 273 months. In the other 5, the airline networks add up to 3,671 more flights in total; where one airline's whole network equals the gap, it is named:

| Month | National row | Sum of airline networks | Gap | Gap = network of |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mar 2006 | 39,573 | 39,751 | 178 | no single airline |
| May 2023 | 45,681 | 46,089 | 408 | Skytrans |
| Mar 2024 | 46,425 | 47,384 | 959 | Hinterland |
| Apr 2024 | 46,511 | 47,526 | 1,015 | Hinterland |
| May 2024 | 46,232 | 47,343 | 1,111 | Hinterland |

Where a single airline explains the gap (Skytrans (1 month; first reported May 2023), Hinterland (3 months; first reported Mar 2024)), it is always in its first three months of reporting: a new airline left out of the industry total at first. A national KPI built from the airline rows and one taken from the national row will disagree in these months, so the marts have to pick one and say so.
